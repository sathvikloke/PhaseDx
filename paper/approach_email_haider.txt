Subject: A pixel-blind baseline reads 0.85 per slice and 0.51 per patient on a prostate MRI benchmark — and why PI-CAI got its evaluation unit right

Dear Dr Haider,

On the fastMRI Prostate T2 benchmark, scored on its own published split, a model that
reads no pixels — only each slice's relative position within the acquisition — reaches
AUC 0.854 per slice and 0.506 per patient from the identical score vector. A constant
predictor reads exactly 0.500 at both units.

The reason I am writing to you rather than to a general radiologist is what happened when
we applied the same locked protocol to PI-CAI. We read its 1,295 human-expert lesion
delineation volumes and found the same divergence: 0.748 per slice against 0.571 per
patient over 24 holdouts. That result is an argument that PI-CAI's designers chose
correctly. The challenge is scored at the case level, which is the unit our finding
says is the defensible one; the slice-level number simply is not available to be inflated
because nobody scores it that way. We do not criticise PI-CAI anywhere in the paper, and
I would rather say that in the first paragraph than have you wonder.

I am asking you because you are a PI-RADS v2.1 co-author who publishes cautionary results
under your own name — the decision-curve paper finding a well-performing CNN was
miscalibrated into net harm, and the 2025 domain-shift result using PI-CAI itself. Those
are direct evidence that you will engage with a null result rather than resist it.

What is already done. The analysis is finished and the code is public. Every value
sits on a subject-disjoint split with a single fit; the flagship is reproduced by a second
implementation sharing no code with the first; and we obtained the official label file for
the largest benchmark and reconciled our slice-ordering source against it row for row,
with zero disagreements across 4.5 million label comparisons.

What I am asking. Co-authorship, on the ICMJE criteria, for contributions I cannot
make:

1. Whether a positional prior on prostate MRI is better described as a confound or simply
   as the anatomy of where clinically significant cancer sits in the gland — and where the
   paper should draw that line.
2. What a slice-level csPCa metric would mean in an actual reading workflow. The PI-CAI
   arm is new and currently carries no clinical interpretation at all.
3. Critical revision of the clinical framing, which is presently written by people who do
   not read scans.

Two things you should know before deciding. My co-authors and I are secondary-school
students; there is no funding and no institutional sponsor. And I am separately
approaching a neuroradiologist about the intracranial hemorrhage arm, since the paper
spans two subspecialties and I do not think one person should be asked to speak for both.

The manuscript is attached. The target is *Radiology: Artificial Intelligence* and it is
not yet submitted, so your name would go on before submission rather than being added
afterwards.

If the answer is no, a single paragraph on question 1 would still be worth a great deal,
and I would acknowledge it rather than list you as an author. If you would rather not
spend time on this at all, please say so and I will not follow up.

With thanks for your time,
