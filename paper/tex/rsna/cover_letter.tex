Benchmark Certifies without the Pixels: An Audit of Seven Public Imaging

\subsection*{3. The central construction is prior art, and it is not ours}

Fitting a model to a slice's position in its stack, with no image data, and
comparing it with a published slice-level number, has been done before. The
manuscript says so in its Introduction rather than in a late paragraph of the
Discussion.

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.2em}
\item A public Kaggle notebook, ``Baseline with no image'' (RSNA-STR Pulmonary
Embolism Detection, October 2020), bins the label rate against relative slice
position on the training set and applies it to the test set with no pixels.
That is the same construction, six years ago.
\item Yan et al (CVPR 2018), in the paper defining the DeepLesion lesion-type
task, report a ``Baseline: Location feature'' row at 59.7\% against their own
method's 90.5\%. A location-only baseline on that benchmark is theirs.
\item Badgeley et al (\emph{npj Digital Medicine} 2019) showed hip-fracture
performance collapsing to AUC 0.52 on a confounder-balanced test set.
\item Yagis et al (2021) and Tampu et al (2022) measured the inflation caused
by slice-level evaluation.
\end{itemize}

What we add is narrower than the construction, and we would rather state it
narrowly than have a reviewer find the boundary for us:

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.2em}
\item \textbf{A result that needs no published comparator.} On the RSNA 2019
Intracranial Hemorrhage benchmark, one pixel-blind score vector reads AUC 0.737
(95\% CI: 0.735, 0.740) per slice and 0.453 (95\% CI: 0.445, 0.461) per
patient, on all six official labels. The comparison is between two readings of
the same score vector, so it does not depend on any published number being
correct. Four independent routes, one an independent reimplementation sharing
no code, agree to 0.003.
\item \textbf{A measurement under a correct split.} The existing literature on
the evaluation unit measures the cost of a \emph{wrong}, leaky split. Every
value here was obtained under a patient-disjoint one, with intervals that
resample subjects rather than slices.
\item \textbf{A distribution rather than a verdict.} Against peer-reviewed
comparators, the median share of the published margin over chance reachable
with no pixels is 0.469 (IQR: 0.437, 0.490; $n=9$ benchmark-arms).
\end{itemize}

That last number also bounds what we may claim, and the Discussion says so: a
median near one half is not a demonstration that published slice-level results
are accounted for by trivial baselines. One of the nine arms returns
$-0.002$ --- none of the margin --- and is reported at the same prominence as
the rest. No peer-reviewed comparison in the paper is matched; the only rows
reaching parity have a preprint comparator and are labeled as such wherever
they appear. Every claim is about an evaluation protocol. We make no claim
about what any published model learned, which no analysis of a label file could
support.

\subsection*{Subject overlap}

None of the authors has previously published on any patient, animal or
experiment reported here, and none of us has related work under review or in
press.

The patients themselves have all been reported before, and necessarily so: this
is a retrospective secondary analysis of public label files, so every subject
was first reported by the group that released the benchmark, and many were
reported again by the comparator papers whose published numbers we use as
denominators. Those releases and comparator papers are cited in Materials and
Methods and in Results. No new patient data were collected, no identifiable
information was used, and no pixel data were downloaded.

\subsection*{One point where the journal's requirements pull against each other}

withheld for review and supplied on acceptance. The archive is already public
