{\large\bfseries What a Slice-Level Benchmark Certifies without the Pixels:
An Audit of Seven Public Imaging Datasets\par}

\vspace{0.8em}

\textbf{Article Type:} Original Research

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Summary Statement}

\textbf{One score vector computed with no access to the images ranked slices far
above chance and patients at or below it on the largest benchmark audited, and
the difference between the two readings was carried by how many slices a
patient's stack held.}

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Key Points}

\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.1em}
\item On a frozen holdout of \num{5681} patients, one pixel-blind score vector
read 0.738 (95\% confidence interval: 0.733, 0.742) per slice and, after
pre-specified mean aggregation, 0.442 (95\% confidence interval: 0.428, 0.458)
per patient, while a constant predictor read exactly 0.500 at both units.
\item The patient-level reading is a stack-depth confound of the mean operator:
stack depth alone reads 0.394 per patient, and with depth held fixed the reading
is 0.5071 (95\% confidence interval: 0.5013, 0.5129), sitting on its own
permutation null of 0.5055 rather than at chance.
\item Against the strongest peer-reviewed comparator on each of four benchmarks,
the same locked pixel-blind baseline reached 0.491, 0.480 and $-0.002$ of the
published margin over chance on the three where it is estimable; the spread, not
an average, is the finding.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Abbreviations}

AUC = area under the receiver operating characteristic curve;
CI = confidence interval;
DWI = diffusion-weighted imaging;
ICH = intracranial hemorrhage;
SD = standard deviation.

\vspace{0.4em}

\textbf{Keywords:} CT; MRI; Computer Applications---Detection/Diagnosis;
Technology Assessment; Brain/Brain Stem.

\section*{Abstract}

\textbf{Purpose:} To measure what a model given no image data reaches on
volumetric benchmarks read slice by slice, and what the same scores show per
patient.

\textbf{Materials and Methods:} Retrospective secondary analysis of
de-identified label files from seven imaging benchmarks, including NYU fastMRI,
analyzed in 2026. The largest, the 2019 RSNA Intracranial
Hemorrhage benchmark, held \num{752802} slices from \num{18938} patients; age and
sex are not distributed. A 20-bin relative-position model was locked as the
primary pixel-blind baseline before any held-out value was computed, fitted once
on \num{13257} patients, and read on a frozen patient-disjoint holdout of the
remaining \num{5681} at both units, with mean aggregation pre-specified for the
patient unit. Uncertainty is a 95\% percentile bootstrap confidence
interval (CI) resampling patients and the spread over 24 independently drawn holdouts.

\textbf{Results:} One score vector read area under the receiver operating
characteristic curve (AUC) 0.738 (95\% CI: 0.733, 0.742) per slice and 0.442
(95\% CI: 0.428, 0.458) per patient; over 24 holdouts, 0.7381 and 0.4551; a
constant predictor read exactly 0.500 at both units. Gaps over six labels ran
0.181--0.318. Stack depth alone read 0.394 per patient; with depth fixed the
reading was 0.5071 (95\% CI: 0.5013, 0.5129), 0.0016 above its own permutation
null of 0.5055. It reached 0.491, 0.480 and $-0.002$ of three peer-reviewed
benchmarks' published margins over chance; on a fourth it was not estimable.

\textbf{Conclusion:} A pixel-blind score vector ranked slices far above chance
and patients at or below it, the difference carried by stack depth.

\section*{Introduction}

A three-dimensional acquisition is often labeled slice by slice, and performance
is then reported by pooling slices into a single area under the receiver
operating characteristic curve (AUC). The clinical question is almost always
about a patient. The gap is arithmetic: findings are not uniformly distributed
along an organ, so a slice-level ranking can be dominated by \emph{where} a slice
sits in the stack, and a score vector encoding only that geometry ranks slices
well and patients not at all.

None of this is new. Shortcut learning is named \citep{geirhos2020shortcut}:
Badgeley et al predicted hip fracture at AUC 0.78 from the radiograph, 0.91 with
hospital process features, and 0.52---chance---on a test set balanced across
those variables \citep{badgeley2019hip}. On the evaluation unit, slice-level
cross-validation inflated test accuracy by 29\%--55\% and reached about 96\% on
\emph{randomly labeled} data \citep{yagis2021leakage,tampu2022leakage}. Stronger
baselines have been urged \citep{varoquaux2022failures}.

The central construction used here is prior art. A public 2020 Kaggle notebook
on the RSNA-STR Pulmonary Embolism Detection challenge, ``Baseline with no
image,'' bins the label rate against relative slice position on the training set
and applies it to the test set with no pixels \citep{osciiart2020baseline}. Yan
et al, defining the DeepLesion lesion-type task, publish a ``Baseline: Location
feature'' row at 59.7\% eight-class accuracy against their own method's 90.5\%
\citep{yan2018deeplesiongraphs}. Closest of all, Wallis and Buvat classify
tumours in a widely used brain MRI dataset to high accuracy \emph{without
information about the tumour}, tracing it to implicit radiologist input in the
selection of the 2-D slices \citep{wallis2022cleverhans}---a shortcut carried by
which slices were chosen, where the one studied here is carried by where a slice
sits among those chosen.

That literature measures the cost of a \emph{wrong}, leaky split rather than what
survives a \emph{correct}, patient-disjoint one, and it does not ask what one
pixel-blind score vector is worth when the unit of the reading changes. The
purpose of this study was to measure one such vector at both units under a
patient-disjoint split, to identify the mechanism separating them, and to
describe how much of a published margin over chance it reaches.

\section*{Materials and Methods}

\subsection*{Study Design}

This was a retrospective secondary analysis of publicly released, de-identified
label files from imaging benchmarks released 2016--2024; analyses ran in July and
August 2026. Institutional review board approval was not required: no data were
obtained through interaction with any living individual and no identifiable
private information was used. Age and sex are not distributed with the flagship
label file.

\subsection*{Benchmark Eligibility}

A dataset entered the audit if the four fields a positional null needs---subject
identifier, slice index or $z$ position, per-slice label, and train/test
assignment---were obtainable without pixel data or a data-use agreement. Of 25
candidates, eight label files across seven benchmarks were used, in two tiers:
four \emph{core}, with every field in the benchmark's own pixel-free release, and
four \emph{qualified}, carried for a named purpose with one field absent or
reachable only outside it. The flagship is qualified, not core (Table 1).

The seven are RSNA 2019 Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH)
\citep{flanders2020rsnaich}, fastMRI Prostate, T2-weighted and
diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) arms \citep{tibrewala2023fastmriprostate},
DeepLesion \citep{yan2018deeplesion}, fastMRI+ knee \citep{zhao2022fastmriplus},
Duke Breast Cancer MRI \citep{saha2021dukebreastmri,saha2018radiogenomics},
PI-CAI \citep{saha2024picai} and LUNA16 \citep{setio2017luna16}; RSNA ICH is the
largest, at \num{752802} slices, \num{21744} series and \num{18938} patients.

Every RSNA ICH value rests on a slice ordering and a subject identifier its
official release does not contain; both came from a public, license-permissive,
pixel-free tabular mirror \citep{burduja2020ich}. Neither was assumed. Table 2
reports the internal-consistency tests that validate the mapping, the power of
each, and what each cannot detect. No image header was read, so they can falsify
a bad mapping and never certify a good one.

\subsection*{The Zero-Image Baseline Family}

The \emph{primary} baseline was locked before any held-out value was computed: a
\emph{positional} model, a 20-bin estimate of the label rate given relative slice
position within a volume. Four secondary models were fit on the same training
rows: volume size; a depth-limited tree over the remaining non-image columns; a
combined position-plus-metadata tree; and each admissible column read singly. A
constant prevalence predictor is the chance anchor; columns that restate the
label, or derive from the images, were excluded. Non-image columns are classified
three ways, because they are not interchangeable: geometry and acquisition,
administrative, and legitimate clinical predictors. The primary result rests on
the first class alone, the only class the flagship label file contains.

\subsection*{Estimator, Aggregation, Intervals, and Nulls}

Every baseline produces one score vector per test set, read at \emph{both}
units: slice-level AUC, and patient-level AUC after aggregating each subject's
slice scores. The \emph{mean} operator was pre-specified; maximum, top-$k$ mean
and quantile operators are sensitivity analyses, reported with their number of
distinct patient scores.

Splits are subject-disjoint, using a benchmark's published assignment where one
exists. On RSNA ICH, which publishes none, the primary estimator is \emph{one
frozen patient-disjoint holdout}: 30\% of patients at a pre-set seed, a single
fit on the rest, no pooling (Table 3 note). A constant
predictor therefore scores exactly 0.500 at both units by construction, which is
the point of the design. The pooled out-of-fold estimator used in an earlier
round does not---each fold emits its own training prevalence, so fold identity
becomes rankable, putting its constant predictor at 0.492 per slice---and is
retained only as a labeled sensitivity analysis and on four arms that could not
be recomputed.

Two uncertainty statements accompany every primary value: a 95\% percentile
bootstrap CI resampling \emph{patients} over 2000 replicates, conditioning on
the one fit and the one holdout; and the across-holdout spread, which repeats
draw, fit and score over 24 holdouts and conditions on neither. Both are
reported. Each baseline is read against its own permutation null, which for the
positional model shuffles labels \emph{within each series}, preserving
prevalence, subject clustering and stack depth.

\subsection*{The Comparison Statistic}

\begin{equation*}
\text{trivial fraction}=\frac{\text{locked zero-image baseline}-\text{chance}}
{\text{published}-\text{chance}}
\end{equation*}

with chance $=0.5$ for AUC and the majority-class rate for multiclass accuracy,
against each benchmark's \emph{strongest} published system. The numerator is the
locked baseline, so it carries no selection optimism. It is \emph{descriptive},
not inferential: baseline and comparator sit on different splits and, across
benchmarks, on different metrics against different chance anchors, so no average
is taken. Values outside $[0,1]$ are reported unclipped.

\subsection*{Statistical Analysis}

This is an estimation study: no significance test was pre-specified and no $P$
values are reported. Replicate count and seed are recorded per run. Analyses used
Python 3.14 (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, Del) with NumPy 2.4, pandas
3.0 and, for the tree baselines, scikit-learn 1.8.

\section*{Results}

\subsection*{One Locked Score Vector, Read at Two Units}

No published number enters this section. On RSNA ICH the locked 20-bin
positional model was fitted once on
\num{13257} patients and read on the frozen holdout of the remaining \num{5681}
(\num{226663} slices, \num{32258} positive; \num{2306} positive patients).
Read at the slice level the resulting vector reaches AUC 0.738 (95\% CI: 0.733,
0.742); read at the patient level, after the pre-specified mean aggregation, the
same vector reaches 0.442 (95\% CI: 0.428, 0.458). Nothing changes between the
readings except the unit; the difference is 0.295, and over the six official
labels the gaps run 0.181 to 0.318 (Table 2, Fig 1A). A constant predictor
reaches exactly 0.500 at both units, on all six labels and in all 24 holdouts.

At the patient unit the six labels do not all behave alike. Three intervals lie
wholly below chance (any, subarachnoid, subdural) and three cross it; only
epidural's point estimate is above chance, 0.528 (95\% CI: 0.472,
0.579) on 108 positive patients. The within-series permutation null is 0.505 per
slice, so the slice-level excess is 0.233; the same null is 0.580 per patient,
\emph{above} chance, so the observed 0.442 sits 0.138 below what this estimator
reaches with nothing to find.

\subsection*{The Mechanism Is Stack Depth}

A patient's mean is one fitted 20-value curve sampled at that patient's slice
positions, so it is largely a function of how many slices the stack holds; and
depth tracks the label here, reading 0.394 (95\% CI: 0.380, 0.409) per patient on
its own---the direction the mean inherits. Held at fixed depth the anti-ranking
goes: 0.5071 (95\% CI: 0.5013, 0.5129) within exact stack depth, over 29
informative strata, and 0.5024 (95\% CI: 0.4867, 0.5178) within 5-slice strata
(Table 2, Fig 1B). That
interval excludes 0.500 and is not read as chance; what it does is sit at its own
null, the within-series permutation null at fixed depth being 0.5055, 0.0016
below the observed value. Per label the depth-conditioned reading runs 0.4999 to 0.5108.

\subsection*{Pipeline Uncertainty over 24 Holdouts}

Repeating the whole procedure on 24 independently drawn holdouts gives a slice-level mean
of 0.7381 (SD 0.0016; range, 0.7350 to 0.7415), a patient-level mean of 0.4551
(SD 0.0059; range, 0.4424 to 0.4628), a depth-conditioned mean of 0.5037
(SD 0.0021; range, 0.4979 to 0.5071), which covers 0.500, depth alone 0.4031, and a
constant predictor of 0.5000, SD 0.0000
(Table 3, Fig 1C). At the patient unit the bootstrap CI is the wider of the two
here (0.030 against 0.020) and the narrower on two of the six labels, so neither
replaces the other. The frozen holdout is extreme within its own family---lowest of 24 on the
patient reading for three of the six labels, and highest of 24 on the
depth-conditioned reading for two of them---because the depth confound is
unusually strong in it. It is a
reproducible reference point, not a tighter estimate.

\subsection*{Aggregation, and the Rest of the Baseline Family}

Six alternatives to the pre-specified mean were run (Table 3). Maximum and top-1
mean are exactly degenerate under a single fit, all \num{5681} patients taking
one score, and read exactly 0.500. Three read above chance---top-3 mean
0.514, top-5 mean 0.513 and the 90th percentile 0.556, the first and last with
intervals excluding it---so ``at or below chance''
holds for the pre-specified mean and the 75th percentile (0.480) and not for
those three, which take 4, 9 and 13 distinct values across \num{5681} patients
and are therefore near-degenerate readings of stack depth. The strongest
secondary model at the patient unit is volume size, at 0.601; every column in
this label file is a geometry or acquisition field, which is why the primary
result is about geometry alone.

\subsection*{The Same Reading on Every Other Benchmark-Arm}

Recomputed on its own published split, fastMRI Prostate reads slice 0.854 against
patient 0.506 on T2 and 0.851 against 0.424 on DWI, with a constant predictor of
exactly 0.500 on both (intervals in Table 4); there the depth-conditioned reading
is exactly 0.500 with a degenerate interval, because within a depth stratum every
patient's mean is the same number. All eight DeepLesion body-part arms rank above
chance at both units (slice 0.691--0.977, patient 0.642--0.954) and none
diverges, which is what should happen: the label there is the anatomic region, so
relative position is legitimate task information. LUNA16 moves little at either
unit (0.534, 0.581), and on Duke Breast the patient-level value is undefined, all
922 of 922 patients being positive (Table 4, Fig 2). Four arms could not be moved
off the pooled out-of-fold estimator, and their constant predictors read 0.455,
0.483, 0.483 and 0.483 rather than 0.500; the largest deviation, 0.045 on
fastMRI+ knee meniscus tear, is of the same order as that arm's own patient-level
departure from chance and remains an unrepaired defect.

\subsection*{How Much of a Published Margin Is Reachable with No Pixels}

Against the strongest peer-reviewed comparator on each benchmark, the locked
pixel-blind baseline reached 0.491 (95\% CI: 0.482, 0.499) of the published
margin over chance on RSNA ICH, 0.480 on the DeepLesion eight-class lesion-type
task, and $-0.002$ on LUNA16 (Fig 3); over the six hemorrhage labels it runs
0.405 to 0.610. On PI-CAI the quantity is \emph{not estimable}: the locked
baseline needs a slice index the label file does not carry, so the row is
plotted as unavailable, not as zero. The heterogeneity is the result: two of
three estimable benchmarks return roughly half the published margin and one
returns none. No average is taken, because they sit on different metrics against
different chance anchors---slice AUC, eight-class accuracy against 0.2361, and
sensitivity at one false positive per scan against 0.0027. PI-CAI's tree over
four non-image columns reaches 0.692 per patient, but two of those columns are
clinical predictors, so that reading supports no inference about acquisition
confounding. Zero peer-reviewed comparisons are matched: the only rows reaching
parity are a worked example on fastMRI Prostate, 0.981 and 0.973, against a
comparator still unpublished two years after posting
\citep{rempe2024kspace}.

\section*{Discussion}

The unit at which a three-dimensional benchmark is read decides what its number
means. On a frozen patient-disjoint holdout a single pixel-blind score vector
read 0.738 per slice and 0.442 per patient; only the first is what a paper would
print. The mechanism is identified rather than inferred: mean aggregation carries
stack depth into a per-patient score, depth is associated with the label here,
and with depth held fixed the same vector reads 0.5071, on its own permutation
null. Read the patient-level value as a
null, not as a reversal.

Two protocol choices carry that claim. The estimator is a single frozen holdout
with one fit, under which a constant predictor is exactly 0.500 by construction;
the pooled out-of-fold alternative put that floor at 0.492, a ranking artifact of
pooling rather than a measurement. And
the baseline was locked before the held-out data were touched, so nothing in the
primary result is selected on the outcome. The cost is precision: every interval
widens by about 1.7 to 1.9 times. An across-holdout spread is quoted alongside
every bootstrap CI because a bootstrap conditions on one fitted model and one
split, and the spread does not.

The share of a published margin reachable with no pixels is a description of four
benchmarks, not a rate for a field, and not a decomposition: the two models may
exploit the same shortcut, different ones, or overlapping ones. Two return
roughly half, two return none, and the metrics differ, so an average would be an
artifact of the mix. Where a pixel-blind model ranks well and does \emph{not}
diverge between units, the right reading may be that the task is legitimately
positional, as DeepLesion's is.

Badgeley et al's control is \emph{negative}---balance the confounders and watch
the model collapse to 0.52 \citep{badgeley2019hip}. This one is \emph{positive}:
fit a model on the confounder alone and see what it reaches. Against the leakage
literature the distinction is the
split, theirs wrong and this one patient-disjoint
\citep{yagis2021leakage,tampu2022leakage}; against Yan et al it is the source of
the position, theirs from a body-part regressor on the image and this one from a
published column \citep{yan2018deeplesiongraphs}.

For a radiologist reading an imaging AI paper, the operative question is what a
reported AUC was computed over. On the hemorrhage benchmark the slice-pooled
number stood 0.181 to 0.318 above the per-patient number from the same scores,
and the per-patient number is the one aligned with a per-patient decision---a
statement about these benchmarks, since no regulatory filing was examined. For benchmark publishers, three fields
already in the released label file---a subject identifier, a slice index or $z$
position, and the train/test assignment---make every one of these checks runnable
by anyone. What this protocol cannot do is detect shortcuts living in the pixels.

\textbf{Limitations.} The construction is prior art
\citep{osciiart2020baseline,yan2018deeplesiongraphs}. The frozen holdout is
extreme within its own family of 24 on three of the six labels, which is why both
intervals are quoted; the depth-conditioned reading covers 0.500 across that
family while excluding it here. Four audited arms still rest on the
superseded pooled estimator, one with a constant predictor 0.045 from 0.500. The
patient-level reading is not bin-robust, unlike the slice-level one: over 5 to 50
bins it runs 0.434 to 0.632 per patient against 0.717 to 0.745 per slice. Twenty
bins was pre-specified. Two benchmarks are not pixel-free in the same clean
sense: fastMRI+ knee needs slice counts from image file headers, and LUNA16's
candidate list is detector-produced. The PI-CAI comparison is across cohorts, and no peer-reviewed comparison is
matched. Age and sex are absent from the flagship label file, and no subgroup
analysis is reported.

In summary, one pixel-blind score vector ranked slices well above chance and
patients at or below it, the difference carried by stack depth rather than by any
reversal of the positional signal. Three fields a benchmark already
publishes make that checkable by anyone, without the images.
